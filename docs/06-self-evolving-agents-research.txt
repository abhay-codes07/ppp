CHAPTER 6. SELF-EVOLVING AGENTS: WHERE PETRIFLOW STANDS AND WHAT TO RESEARCH
===========================================================================

Method. Web literature search on 10 October 2026. Every paper below was opened at the URL
given (abstract page unless stated). Six of the most-cited items were re-fetched a second
time to confirm title, author and the quoted number. Items marked [snippet] were seen only
in search results. Statements marked [inference] are my reasoning, not a sourced claim.
Full URL list: research/sources.txt.

6.1 What "self-evolving agent" means (two reference surveys)
------------------------------------------------------------
Gao et al., "A Survey of Self-Evolving Agents: What, When, How, and Where to Evolve on the
Path to Artificial Super Intelligence", arXiv:2507.21046 (v1 Jul 2025, v4 Jan 2026; TMLR
01/2026). Framing: WHAT evolves (model weights, context = prompts and memory, tools,
architecture/workflow), WHEN (during a task vs between tasks), HOW (reward-based,
imitation, population/evolutionary). Its open-challenges section has "Safe and
Controllable Self-Evolving Agents" and names automated verification and sandboxing as
future work for tool-creating agents.

Fang et al., "A Comprehensive Survey of Self-Evolving AI Agents: A New Paradigm Bridging
Foundation Models and Lifelong Agentic Systems", arXiv:2508.07407 (Aug 2025). Framing: a
feedback loop of System Inputs -> Agent System -> Environment -> Optimisers, where the
optimisers change prompts, memory, tools, workflows or the model. Companion framework:
EvoAgentX (arXiv:2507.03616).

Working definition: a deployed agent that persistently changes a reusable part of itself
(weights, prompts, memory, tools/skills, workflow topology) from feedback on its own runs,
with little human intervention.

6.3 Safety findings specific to self-evolution (why a static gate matters)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Misevolution: Shao et al., "Your Agent May Misevolve", arXiv:2509.26354 (ICLR 2026).
  Safety decays INSIDE the improvement loop with no attacker, along four pathways: model,
  memory, tool, workflow. Press coverage of the paper reports refusal rates on harmful
  prompts falling from 99.4% to 54.4% in one coding-agent test.
- Experience-driven safety decay: Zhao et al., Findings of ACL 2026 (aclanthology
  2026.findings-acl.2091): benign experience still weakens safety (execution bias); adding
  refusal experience causes over-refusal; a measured safety-utility trade-off.
- Reward hacking of the evidence: the DGM project page's faked test log is the canonical
  example: the agent corrupted the SIGNAL the overseer relied on.
- Auditing self-evolution in finance: arXiv:2608.17684: utility rose 0.741 -> 0.837 while
  injection exposure rose 0.820 -> 0.943; unauthorised state changes 0.685.
- "Safety in Self-Evolving Agents: A Survey", arXiv:2610.00093 (SAVER framework):
  legitimate state becomes unsafe when adaptation extends its persistence, authority or
  scope beyond the conditions under which it was valid; repairing descendant states is the
  least-solved part.
- Verifier tax: Dantas et al., "Toward Safe LLM Agents: A Survey of Specification,
  Verification, and Enforcement", arXiv:2608.14590: "blocking 94% of unsafe actions can
  still result in less than 5% safe task completion because agents exploit alternative
  unsafe paths"; runtime monitoring is the most mature enforcement; NL-to-formal-spec
  translation is the bottleneck. The survey does not mention self-evolving agents or Petri
  nets at all.
- Monitor coverage bound: Zhang, "Why Formal Monitors Fail", arXiv:2608.01388: the recall of
  any fixed-invariant finite-state monitor is bounded by the concentration of the attack
  distribution; 68-75% recall on concentrated attacks, 6-13% on dispersed ones. This is the
  theoretical version of loophole L2 (regex tool mapping).
- "Agent Safety Is Action Alignment", arXiv:2606.28739: argues action safety belongs at the
  action boundary (least privilege on tool calls), outside the weights. That is exactly
  the position a tool gate occupies.

6.4 Formal methods and Petri nets around LLM agents (the neighbourhood PetriFlow lives in)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Petri nets specifically (sparse):
- Czejdo, Daszczuk, Grabski, "Verification of Synchronization of LLM Agents in the
  Integrated Model of Distributed Systems Using Petri Net", DepCoS-RELCOMEX 2026, Springer
  LNNS 2014 pp. 68-79: reachability/deadlock analysis of LLM multi-agent coordination.
- TB-CSPN (Pareschi et al., Future Internet 17(8):363, 2025): coloured Petri nets for
  coordination; LLMs confined to topic extraction. [snippet; page 403]
- adk-libpetri (github.com/debe/adk-libpetri): compiles Google ADK agents into Coloured
  Time Petri nets and proves deadlock-freedom and bounded reachability with Z3 BEFORE
  execution. README: "The proofs are structural and untimed"; early-stage 0.x.
  This is the closest living relative of PetriFlow, and it already does what PetriFlow's
  website claims (a verifier with named properties).
- LLM-as-solver / Petri-net-as-checker: arXiv:2606.29627, arXiv:2604.09318 [snippet].
Runtime enforcement with specifications (no Petri nets, same job as the gate):
- AgentSpec (ICSE 2026, arXiv:2503.18666): trigger-predicate-enforcement DSL in the agent
  loop; >90% unsafe executions prevented in code agents; ms overhead.
- Progent (arXiv:2504.11703): privilege-control DSL at the tool-call boundary; supports
  LLM-generated and DYNAMICALLY UPDATED policies.
- ShieldAgent (ICML 2025, arXiv:2503.22738): rules extracted from policy documents into
  verifiable probabilistic rule circuits.
- VeriGuard (ACL 2026, arXiv:2510.05156): LLM drafts a policy as code, it is tested and
  formally verified offline, then a light monitor enforces it online.
- Agent-C (arXiv:2512.23738): temporal constraints enforced during decoding via SMT.
- AgentLTL (arXiv:2607.02599), ContrAgent (arXiv:2609.18128, LTLf contracts compiled to
  DFAs that gate tool calls), CaMeLoT (arXiv:2609.18674, CTL model checking of a plan with
  nuXmv before any tool runs, counterexample-guided repair), ProbGuard (arXiv:2508.00500).

6.6 How far has PetriFlow gone toward self-evolving agents? Not at all, by design.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Grep of the repo for evolv, self-improv, learn, adapt, improve, feedback, mutate, optimi:
no hits in code except layout "feedback edges" in the viewer and doc wording. The rules are
compiled once and enforced unchanged. The only runtime dynamism is: addNet/removeNet from a
fixed registry, expandWorkflow/injectTokens in the engine, and a DecisionProvider that lets
an LLM choose WHICH ENABLED transition fires (never which transitions exist). The author's
own blog says: rules compiled and verified at build time, enforced unchanged at runtime.

6.7 What can be concluded from this repo for self-evolving-agent work
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Sourced conclusions:
C1 The two literatures are disjoint. Self-evolution papers change the agent; guardrail
   papers hold a spec fixed. The specification/verification survey never mentions
   self-evolution; the self-evolution surveys mention verification only as future work.
   A static Petri-net gate is ORTHOGONAL to the frontier, not behind it.
C2 The evolving substrate (memory, self-built tools, skills, workflow) is exactly where
   safety erodes (misevolution, ACL 2026, finance audit). The one thing those papers lack is
   a component the loop cannot vote away. A tool gate outside the agent's edit scope is
   that component. [last sentence: inference]
C3 Three results bound what any such gate can claim: entropy-bounded recall of fixed
   monitors (L2 in theory), the verifier tax (blocking is not the same as safe completion),
   and the DGM faked log (an agent can corrupt the evidence the gate relies on; L6 is the
   PetriFlow instance: forged history).
C4 PetriFlow's actual contribution is small and concrete: a clean 4-phase runtime gate,
   deferred transitions, and a DSL whose five templates compose by AND. Its verification
   story is the weakest part and is the first thing a researcher should fix.

Research directions that connect the two (ranked by how much the repo already supports):
R1 Rules as the evolvable artefact under a verifier. Let a self-improving agent propose
   .rules edits; accept only if (a) each net still enumerates, (b) the COMPOSED net is
   deadlock-free and every tool is reachable, (c) a stated property set still holds.
   Progent and VeriGuard already do (c) for their own DSLs. Needed in PetriFlow: composed
   analysis and a property language (neither exists today).
R2 Monotone safety across self-modification. AND-composition means adding a rule only
   removes behaviours: a provable one-way ratchet. The open problem is relaxing or removing
   a rule safely (SAVER's "repair descendant states"). Formalise: when may a net leave the
   registry without making a previously unreachable bad state reachable?
R3 Reachability output as an evolution signal. LeanEvolve and CaMeLoT use verifier output
   to drive repair; AgentLTL uses spec compliance as RL reward. The gate's block events and
   the analyser's terminal-state report are cheap dense signals for an AFlow/DGM-style
   search over workflows.
R4 Evidence integrity. Both DGM's faked logs and L6 show the signal can be forged. Research:
   gate state derived only from server-observed tool results, signed histories, or a
   decision log that the agent cannot write.
R5 Data-aware rules (coloured Petri nets) so that "backup OF X before delete OF X" is in the
   net, not in JavaScript; bounded colour domains keep enumeration finite. Removes L1.
R6 Classification beyond regex (L2): shell AST parsing, sandbox syscall observation, or a
   small classifier with the net as backstop; measure false negatives against the entropy
   bound of arXiv:2608.01388.
R7 Guard-aware analysis (SMT over filtrex guards) so --strict stops over-approximating.
R8 Measure the claim behind 0.3.0: do marking-style block messages improve recovery and
   compliance across models, and do they help an injected model find the unlock path?

6.8 Suggested one-paragraph position for the meeting
----------------------------------------------------
PetriFlow is a tool-call gate: a fixed, pre-enumerated set of tiny Petri nets that allow or
block tool names by order. It does not evolve and does not try to. The current research on
self-evolving agents shows safety eroding precisely in the parts that evolve, which makes a
fixed gate outside the agent's reach more relevant, not less. But to serve that role the
gate needs the three things it lacks: analysis of the composed policy, a property language
so "verified" means a checked claim, and evidence integrity so the agent cannot forge the
history the gate trusts. Those three are a concrete research agenda, and this repo is a
small, readable base to start from.
